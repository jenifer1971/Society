BMW GROUP — COMPANY PROFILE AND NEWS 2023–2026
==============================================

COMPANY OVERVIEW

BMW Group is a German multinational manufacturer of luxury automobiles and motorcycles, headquartered in Munich, Germany. The group comprises three automotive brands — BMW, MINI, and Rolls-Royce — and the BMW Motorrad motorcycle brand. BMW Group is publicly listed on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange (BMW.DE). The Quandt family holds approximately 46% of shares.

In 2023, BMW Group delivered 2.55 million vehicles, including 375,000 fully electric vehicles (BEV), representing 15% of total deliveries. Group revenue reached €155.2 billion, and pre-tax profit was €17.1 billion, among the highest in the company's history. BMW outperformed most peers on profitability during the transition period.

ELECTRIFICATION STRATEGY

BMW's EV strategy differentiated itself from Volkswagen Group's approach: rather than developing an EV-exclusive platform, BMW opted for flexible architectures capable of supporting ICE, hybrid, and BEV powertrains on the same production lines. This approach offered manufacturing flexibility but faced criticism from analysts who argued it would limit EV optimization.

BMW's current electric lineup in 2023–2024:
- BMW i4: electric version of the 4 Series Gran Coupé, top-selling BMW BEV
- BMW iX3: electric version of the X3 SUV, built in China for global markets
- BMW iX: flagship dedicated BEV SUV, positioned as a technology showcase
- BMW i5: electric version of the 5 Series
- BMW i7: electric version of the 7 Series flagship sedan
- MINI Cooper Electric, MINI Countryman Electric
- BMW iX1, iX2: compact SUV BEVs

BMW committed to making 50% of global deliveries fully electric by 2030.

2023: RECORD PROFITS, EV MOMENTUM

BMW Group reported record revenues and profits in 2023 despite global economic uncertainty. The luxury vehicle segment proved more resilient to interest rate increases than mass-market brands. Order backlogs, built during the semiconductor shortage, continued to support favorable pricing well into 2023.

BMW's iX and i4 models received strong critical acclaim and sold well in Europe, the United States, and China. The i4 M50 and iX M60 high-performance variants demonstrated that EV performance could match or exceed BMW's traditional M-Sport heritage.

BMW announced a €1.7 billion investment to expand its Leipzig plant in Germany for production of the upcoming Neue Klasse electric vehicles.

In China, BMW maintained its position as the leading premium automotive brand by sales. The BMW-Brilliance joint venture (BMW holding 75%) remained highly profitable. BMW committed to investing RMB 15 billion in China through 2025, including a new R&D center in Shenyang and upgraded plant facilities.

BMW partnered with CATL, Samsung SDI, and EVE Energy for battery cell supply, maintaining a multi-supplier strategy to ensure supply security and competitive pricing.

NEUE KLASSE — NEXT GENERATION EV PLATFORM

BMW's most strategically important announcement was the Neue Klasse architecture, revealed in concept form at IAA 2023. Neue Klasse is BMW's first truly dedicated EV platform, developed from a clean sheet. Key attributes:
- 6th-generation round battery cells developed jointly with BMW and manufactured by multiple cell suppliers
- 800V electrical architecture enabling faster charging
- New central computing architecture reducing the number of electronic control units by 40%
- Designed for integration with Level 3+ autonomous driving capability
- 30% improvement in range, 30% faster charging, and 25% reduction in CO2 during production versus current BMW BEVs

The first Neue Klasse vehicle, a compact SAV (Sports Activity Vehicle, BMW terminology for SUV), was planned to enter production at the new Debrecen, Hungary plant in 2025, followed by a sedan variant in Munich.

2024: SOLID-STATE BATTERY, CHINA CHALLENGES

BMW announced a strategic partnership with Solid Power, a Colorado-based solid-state battery developer, for next-generation battery technology. BMW had co-invested in Solid Power since 2017. In 2024, BMW began testing solid-state battery cells in engineering prototype vehicles, with a target for vehicle integration demonstrations in 2025–2026. Mass production was not expected before 2028–2030.

BMW's China sales came under pressure for the first time in years. Chinese consumers increasingly favored domestic brands for EVs due to perceived software superiority, better value, and nationalist sentiment. BMW's iX3, assembled locally in China, faced direct competition from similarly priced Chinese EVs with more advanced intelligent cockpit features.

BMW responded by accelerating integration of local Chinese technology partners. The company partnered with Alibaba for in-car assistant technology, integrated Chinese navigation and digital services more deeply than any other non-Chinese brand, and hired Chinese software engineers to adapt the operating system for local preferences.

BMW Group announced a partnership with Volkswagen Group subsidiary Audi to jointly develop a common EV platform for certain future model segments, seeking to amortize development costs across higher volumes. The partnership was limited to platform components and did not affect brand differentiation.

2025: NEUE KLASSE PRODUCTION BEGINS

The Neue Klasse production ramp began at the Debrecen plant in Hungary in late 2025. Initial production capacity was planned at 150,000 units per year, with ramp to 350,000+ by 2027. The Munich plant was simultaneously converted to produce Neue Klasse sedans alongside legacy 3 Series.

BMW's BEV deliveries approached 600,000 in 2025, on track toward the 50% BEV target for 2030.

The company's software organization, ADAS and Autonomous Driving Division, demonstrated Level 3 automated highway driving capability for integration in Neue Klasse vehicles beginning in 2026.

BMW's charging network joint venture with Mercedes, ChargeNow (later rebranded HPC Alliance), expanded to over 10,000 ultra-fast chargers across Europe, providing a competitive response to Tesla's Supercharger advantage.

2026 OUTLOOK

BMW's strategic position in 2026 was considered strong relative to most European OEM peers:
- Neue Klasse ramp provided a competitive, purpose-built EV product
- High profitability funded transition investment without debt
- Luxury positioning provided pricing power against Chinese competition
- Multi-region manufacturing (Germany, Hungary, US, China, UK) provided flexibility

Key risks: success of Neue Klasse in competing head-on with Chinese EVs in the C-segment; maintaining China market share against BYD, NIO, and technology companies entering automotive; and achieving software capabilities matching Chinese EV leaders.
