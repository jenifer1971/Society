CONTINENTAL AG — COMPANY PROFILE AND NEWS 2023–2026
====================================================

COMPANY OVERVIEW

Continental AG is a German multinational automotive parts manufacturer headquartered in Hanover, Germany, listed on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange (CON.DE). Continental is the world's fourth-largest automotive supplier by revenue. The company operates through two main groups: Automotive (ADAS, displays, software, brake systems) and ContiTech (industrial rubber and plastics). Continental also has a significant Tires division that is highly profitable and cross-subsidizes the structurally pressured automotive technology business.

In 2023, Continental reported group revenue of approximately €41.4 billion. Automotive Technologies revenue was approximately €19 billion, Tires €14 billion, and ContiTech €6.3 billion. The company employed approximately 200,000 people worldwide.

AUTOMOTIVE TECHNOLOGY PRODUCTS

Continental's automotive technology portfolio includes:
- Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS): cameras, radar, sensor fusion
- Electronic brake systems: MK C2 integrated brake system (IBS) for BEVs
- Displays and HMI: instrument clusters, central displays, head-up displays
- Software and connectivity: vehicle architecture, middleware, OTA platforms
- Electric powertrains: high-voltage connectors, onboard charging systems
- Thermal management: HVAC components for EVs

AUTOMOTIVE SPIN-OFF: VITESCO TECHNOLOGIES

Continental had spun off its powertrain division as Vitesco Technologies in 2021. Vitesco focused on electrification components: high-voltage power electronics, e-motors for hybrid/EV applications, and thermal management. In 2024, Schaeffler AG, a German automotive supplier and bearing manufacturer, acquired Vitesco Technologies in a €3.6 billion deal, creating a combined e-mobility specialist with approximately €25 billion in revenue.

2023: TRANSFORMATION UNDER PRESSURE

Continental's Automotive Technologies division reported operating losses in 2023 as R&D investment for software-defined vehicles exceeded revenues from new product launches. The Tires division's strong profitability — EBIT margins consistently above 14% — offset Automotive losses and supported overall group earnings.

Continental experienced significant software project delays with multiple OEM customers. The company's ADAS camera and software systems for Level 2+ automation faced integration challenges with OEM vehicle architectures, leading to delayed launches and penalty payments to customers in some cases.

CEO Nikolai Setzer announced a comprehensive transformation program targeting €400 million in annual cost savings by 2025, including headcount reductions of approximately 5,400 positions, primarily in the Automotive Technologies division in Germany.

Continental's Tires division performed excellently, with market share gains in premium replacement tires in Europe and North America. The tire business provided financial stability during the automotive restructuring.

2024: STRATEGIC REVIEW, POTENTIAL BREAKUP

Continental's board conducted a strategic review of the group structure throughout 2024, under pressure from activist investors including Flossbach von Storch, who argued the conglomerate structure depressed the valuation of the highly profitable Tires business.

In December 2024, Continental announced it would separate the Automotive Technologies and ContiTech divisions from the Tires division through a stock market listing of the combined Automotive/ContiTech entity by 2026. The separated Automotive entity would be a pure-play automotive supplier free to pursue its transformation strategy, while Tires would operate as an independent premium tire company.

Continental's ADAS division secured a major contract with a leading European OEM for its next-generation front camera system with deep learning-based perception, representing estimated revenue of €1.2 billion over 2026–2031. The company also won contracts with multiple Asian OEMs for its radar sensor platform.

The company's Software House, established to build competitive automotive operating system middleware, made progress in 2024 but remained behind schedule relative to aspirational targets. Continental acknowledged that competing with specialist software companies (Elektrobit, its own subsidiary, was one of the leaders) required a different organizational model.

2025: EXECUTION ON SEPARATION

Continental began preparatory work for the automotive/tire separation. The separated entity was given the working name "Automotive Technologies" and attracted interest from private equity as a potential full acquisition target, given the complexity and cost of a stock market listing.

Continental's ADAS business benefited from increasing regulatory requirements for autonomous emergency braking, lane keeping, and driver monitoring across major markets. Euro NCAP's updated 5-star requirements mandated more sophisticated sensor suites, driving incremental sensor revenue.

The company's integrated brake system — a fully electromechanical brake without traditional hydraulics — became increasingly relevant as BEV platforms eliminated conventional vacuum boosters. Continental secured production contracts with multiple BEV platforms launching in 2026–2027.

Continental's head-up display business grew rapidly as OEMs differentiated through advanced augmented reality HUDs projecting navigation and ADAS information onto the windshield. Continental partnered with WayRay for holographic AR display technology.

2026 OUTLOOK

The separation of Continental's business divisions was expected to complete in 2026. Key questions for investors and management: whether the standalone Automotive Technologies entity could achieve profitability without the cross-subsidy from Tires; whether software investments would yield competitive differentiation against specialized software suppliers; and how the business would adapt to accelerating Chinese competition in displays, sensors, and software.

Continental's relationship with Chinese OEMs remained significant — approximately 30% of Automotive Technologies revenue came from Asia, predominantly China. Maintaining this business against local competitors was a strategic priority.
