AUTOMOTIVE INDUSTRY: KEY PARTNERSHIPS, DEALS, AND COMPETITIVE MOVES 2023–2026
==============================================================================

SUPPLIER-OEM STRATEGIC PARTNERSHIPS

Bosch & Volkswagen Group (2023–2025)
Bosch and Volkswagen Group continued their multi-decade relationship but restructured it for the software-defined vehicle era. Bosch's software subsidiary ETAS collaborated with VW's CARIAD on vehicle operating system middleware and domain controller software. The relationship was complicated by CARIAD's internal difficulties — the subsidiary faced management changes and delays. Bosch positioned itself as a technology provider that could offer software faster and more reliably than OEMs could develop in-house.

Valeo & Siemens eAutomotive (Ongoing)
Valeo's joint venture with Siemens for high-voltage electric motors and inverters continued as a major revenue contributor. The JV supplied e-motors to BMW, Stellantis, and several Chinese OEMs. However, competition from Chinese e-motor specialists including Jing-Jin Electric (JJE) and BYD's internal supplier intensified, particularly in the Chinese market.

BYD & Toyota (2023–2025)
Toyota and BYD's joint venture, BYD Toyota EV Technology (BTET), developed the bZ3 sedan for the Chinese market, combining Toyota's quality engineering with BYD's Blade Battery technology. The model sold modestly in China. The partnership signaled Toyota's willingness to leverage Chinese EV expertise — a significant strategic acknowledgment from a company long skeptical of pure BEV technology.

BMW & Solid Power (Battery Technology)
BMW's long-term investment and partnership with Solid Power for solid-state battery development continued through prototype and testing phases. BMW planned vehicle-level integration tests with solid-state cells in 2025, with potential production readiness contingent on Solid Power achieving manufacturing scale.

Continental & Mobileye (Dissolved 2024)
Continental had historically resold Mobileye's EyeQ chips in packaged camera systems. The relationship wound down as Continental developed proprietary deep-learning perception software running on its own sensor hardware. Mobileye simultaneously pursued a direct OEM relationship strategy, bypassing Tier 1 intermediaries.

CHINESE MANUFACTURER EUROPEAN PARTNERSHIPS

BYD & Stellantis (Explored, 2024)
Stellantis CEO Carlos Tavares explored a commercial partnership with BYD in 2024, initially focused on BYD supplying affordable EV models under the Stellantis brand for European and emerging markets. The discussions reflected Stellantis's need for competitive electric platforms at affordable price points. A formal supply agreement for a Citroën/Opel-branded entry EV built on a BYD platform was reported to be under negotiation.

Volkswagen & SAIC (Under Review 2024–2025)
Volkswagen's joint venture with SAIC, China's largest JV, came under strategic review. VW's market share in China fell from 20%+ historically to approximately 14% by 2024 as Chinese EV brands gained share. VW invested in XPENG, a Chinese EV startup, in 2023 to access software technology, and discussed co-developing models for the Chinese market using XPENG's electrical architecture.

Renault & Geely (Horse JV, 2024)
Renault and Geely formed a joint venture called Horse Powertrain to consolidate their internal combustion engine businesses — a recognition that ICE would remain commercially relevant for at least 10–15 more years in global markets even as EVs grew. The JV pooled engineering, manufacturing, and procurement to reduce costs of a declining but still profitable product line.

BATTERY DEALS AND SUPPLY AGREEMENTS

CATL's European Gigafactory (Hungary)
CATL's Debrecen gigafactory in Hungary — the largest battery manufacturing investment in European history at €7.3 billion — began initial production in 2024 with ramp through 2025. The plant supplied BMW, Volkswagen, Mercedes, Stellantis, and other European OEMs, significantly reducing dependence on battery imports from China. Hungary's willingness to attract Chinese investment made it the center of European battery and EV manufacturing.

Samsung SDI & Stellantis (StarPlus Energy)
Samsung SDI and Stellantis established StarPlus Energy, a joint venture battery gigafactory in Kokomo, Indiana, USA. The facility, supported by IRA incentives, targeted 33 GWh initial capacity for supply to Stellantis North American EV models.

LG Energy Solution & Honda (Ohio, USA)
LG Energy Solution and Honda's joint venture battery plant in Jeffersonville, Ohio, targeting 40 GWh annual capacity, continued construction through 2024 with production targeted for 2025. The plant would supply Honda and Acura EVs produced in North America.

COMPETITIVE PRICE WAR — CHINA

Tesla initiated aggressive price cuts in China in January 2023, triggering a market-wide price war. BYD, NIO, Li Auto, XPeng, and traditional OEM brands all reduced prices or improved value propositions. Chinese consumers, already accustomed to technology product price declines, increasingly delayed purchases anticipating further reductions. This dynamic pressured margins across the industry.

By 2024, approximately 50 Chinese NEV brands were competing aggressively, with analysts predicting consolidation to fewer than 10 viable independent brands by 2030. Companies with vertical integration (BYD), technology platform advantages (Huawei ecosystem), or deep-pocketed backers (NIO with Middle Eastern sovereign wealth) were considered most likely to survive.

TARIFF AND TRADE DYNAMICS

The EU-China tariff escalation, US 100% tariffs on Chinese EVs, and Canada's decision to follow the US with its own tariffs on Chinese EVs created a fragmented global market by 2025. Chinese manufacturers pursued local production in tariff-free geographies (Hungary, Turkey, Morocco, Brazil, Thailand) to access major markets. European and American trade policy uncertainty complicated long-term investment planning for all parties.
